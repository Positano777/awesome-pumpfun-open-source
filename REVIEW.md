# Build Credibility Through Verifiable Open Source

**Positano777 should build a curated Solana/pump.fun catalog and one evidence-based launch-analysis tool, rather than mass-fork public repositories.** The supplied shortlist contains useful leads, but several advertised open-source projects have proprietary licenses, missing licenses, or screenshots instead of implementation. The strongest concrete detector base found is **bobvasic/rughawk**: it has an MIT license, real creation-block analysis, regression tests, and recent activity. Its paid hosted plans do not make its MIT source paywalled. Consequently, the claim that nobody has shipped an open-source launch/bundle detector is unsupported. The opportunity is a more transparent, reproducible analyzer with honest uncertainty and demonstrable improvements. Trade execution belongs in the community catalog too, with **Kelows/million** and official Pump SDK packages shortlisted for further review.

Research reference date: **21 October 2026**. This is a practical discovery and source-triage exercise, not an exhaustive inventory, security audit, or runtime validation. No candidate was installed, tested, connected to a wallet, or used to trade. Activity dates below are observed pushes or cached history, not guarantees of ongoing maintenance; cached pages sometimes lag live metadata. The accompanying `pumpfun-repository-catalog.csv` includes every distinct named candidate identified in the research, including unresolved names and package-only leads.

## The supplied shortlist needs substantial correction

**Publicly readable code is not automatically open source.** The current LICENSE files of **nirholas/pump-fun-sdk**, **nirholas/pumpkit**, **nirholas/kol-quest**, and **nirholas/pumpfun-creator-rewards** explicitly restrict reuse without permission. In pump-fun-sdk, the package declares Apache-2.0 while the LICENSE is proprietary; that contradiction is a reason to hold reuse, not choose the more convenient label. The creator-rewards history explicitly records an all-rights-reserved change in July 2026. These repositories remain useful product references, but current branches should not be presented as permissive fork bases. This finding concerns those repositories individually, not all work by their author. ([SDK LICENSE](https://github.com/nirholas/pump-fun-sdk/blob/main/LICENSE), [Pumpkit LICENSE](https://github.com/nirholas/pumpkit/blob/main/LICENSE), [kol-quest LICENSE](https://github.com/nirholas/kol-quest/blob/main/LICENSE), [rewards LICENSE](https://github.com/nirholas/pumpfun-creator-rewards/blob/main/LICENSE), [rewards history](https://github.com/nirholas/pumpfun-creator-rewards/commits/main/))

## RugHawk changes the flagship opportunity

**RugHawk is the first project to inspect deeply, contribute to, or selectively fork.** Its MIT source includes a TypeScript/Next.js app and creation-slot analysis that requests the actual block, examines successful mint-related transactions, calculates buyer ownership deltas, and distinguishes deployer outflows. The repository was observed pushed on **19 October 2026**. Its self-host instructions coexist with hosted free and paid tiers. A subscription for hosted infrastructure is different from a restriction on using MIT code. ([repository](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk), [license](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk/blob/main/LICENSE), [launch implementation](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk/blob/main/lib/scan/launch.ts), [metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/bobvasic/rughawk))

The pasted assessment also treats historical accuracy issues as current failures. RugHawk documents an earlier deployment that mislabeled **11 of 24 outflows** and describes a subsequent accuracy pass; inspected regression cases cover rent refunds, sales, locks, burns, and moved tokens. Those tests were not executed in this research, and author-reported accuracy is not independent validation. The remaining source-level concern is interpretive: creation fee payer is a heuristic for developer identity, and same-block purchases are sometimes described too confidently as insider/bundle evidence. Those are useful targets for an attributed contribution. ([accuracy account](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk#validation-production-2026-10-07), [regression tests](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk/blob/main/tests/engine.test.ts), [launch source](https://github.com/bobvasic/rughawk/blob/main/lib/scan/launch.ts))

([toolkit detector](https://github.com/smokyrudeboys/onchain-agent-toolkit/blob/main/src/bundle-cluster-detector.js), [Rug-Killer detector](https://github.com/drixindustries/Rug-Killer-On-Solana/blob/main/server/services/bundle-detector.ts))

**Call the flagship a “launch evidence analyzer,” with bundle suspicion as one output.** Jito bundles contain up to five sequential, atomic transactions; Solana `getBlock` provides block transactions and metadata, not a standardized Jito bundle identifier. Same-slot buying, a tip, or a shared funder alone cannot establish common control or prove a Jito bundle. `getBundleStatuses` takes known bundle IDs; it does not document a universal historical signature-to-bundle search. Show observed creation-slot co-buying, inferred coordination, and externally corroborated membership as separate fields. ([Jito documentation](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/), [Solana getBlock](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/getblock))

The useful MVP accepts a mint without connecting a wallet, establishes its creation transaction and slot, reconstructs successful buys in that block, measures launch-slot concentration, classifies creator-associated outflows, and returns linked transaction evidence plus JSON. Missing archive history must produce **unknown or incomplete**, never a clean score. Slot and transaction position should express ordering; block-level seconds cannot substantiate invented millisecond timing. Original fixture sets, decoder coverage, false-positive measurement, and explicit attribution limits would create a defensible contribution.

## Reuse protocol infrastructure and include execution deliberately

For protocol truth, start with **pump-fun/pump-public-docs** and its IDLs. Official documentation points to `@pump-fun/pump-sdk` **4.1.0**, `@pump-fun/pump-swap-sdk` **2.1.0**, and a Rust client, alongside recent quote-chain and reserve/fee changes. These details matter to both historical decoding and current trades. The advertised SDK GitHub repositories returned 404 during research, so distinguish an installable official package from a verified public repository suitable for forking. Inspect the exact package payload, license, dependencies, and current instruction behavior before adoption. ([official docs](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs), [reserve/fee changes](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/VIRTUAL_QUOTE_RESERVES_FEE_ADJUSTMENT.md), [Pump SDK package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@pump-fun/pump-sdk), [PumpSwap SDK package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@pump-fun/pump-swap-sdk))

Carbon’s actual release history and Yellowstone’s explicit licensing split strengthen their candidacy beyond README promises. Neither supplies free hosted RPC, guaranteed retention, or a completed launch-risk model. “Free open-source tool” should mean usable source and self-hosting, with infrastructure requirements published plainly. ([Carbon releases](https://github.com/sevenlabs-hq/carbon/releases), [Yellowstone licensing](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc/blob/master/LICENSING.md), [Yellowstone releases](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc/releases))

For **trade execution**, shortlist **Kelows/million** as an application to evaluate and the official SDK packages as instruction-building dependencies. Million documents paper trading by default and manually enabled live copy trading; its MIT license and real application structure make it a better candidate than unidentified “smart-money-shadow.” It is explicitly experimental, and this review did not validate execution correctness, custody, dependencies, or tests. Before publishing an adapter, review signing and external requests, pin dependencies, simulate representative transactions, and document slippage, limits, fees, failure handling, and an immediate disable control. Keep a read-only scan independent of signing. These are implementation acceptance criteria, not a request to connect a funded wallet. ([million](https://github.com/Kelows/million), [MIT license](https://github.com/Kelows/million/blob/main/LICENSE))

Creator-fee accounting can become a later transparency module, but the named proprietary rewards app is not the code base to copy. Its README also admits that pooled social withdrawals cannot always be assigned to a particular mint. An honest treasury view should separate configured recipients, actual paid distributions, balances, expenses, and unresolved attribution. Current fee-share mutability and lock semantics need verification against current programs before any treasury configuration; the reviewed documentation snapshots conflict. ([rewards attribution limits](https://github.com/nirholas/pumpfun-creator-rewards#readme), [official SDK documentation](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@pump-fun/pump-sdk))

## Establish credibility with a catalog and measured contributions

The first repository should be an original **`awesome-pumpfun-open-source`** catalog: clear categories, exact upstream links, license evidence, protocol compatibility notes, infrastructure costs, review dates, and an explicit execution/read-only label. Include held and rejected candidates with reasons so the community can improve the research. Link upstream projects instead of making decorative forks; stars, recent pushes, and the existence of a test directory are discovery signals rather than proof of quality.

The second repository should be a focused **`launch-evidence`** implementation or clearly attributed RugHawk fork. Begin with creation-slot reconstruction and evidence export. Add replayable fixtures for normal launches, independent early buyers, shared infrastructure funders, missing history, failed transactions, transfers, burns, locks, and genuine sales. Preserve upstream notices, identify original changes in the README, and send useful fixes upstream. Publish a small benchmark and limitations before making marketing claims about detecting dishonest launchers.

Execution adapters belong in a later milestone after the analyzer produces reproducible results. Start with paper execution and deterministic instruction/simulation checks, then review opt-in live behavior separately. A subsequent treasury module can publish receipts and expenditure records. Each milestone should ship something a contributor can verify and improve, giving X posts concrete material: a fixed false positive, a decoded protocol change, a fixture contribution, or a measurable reliability result.

The community’s strongest credibility claim is **“our evidence and methods are public.”** A future meme coin should not substitute for maintenance, establish implied investment returns, or turn ordinary same-slot buyers into alleged wrongdoers. Keep the roadmap, contribution process, infrastructure funding, and any future token financing explicit. This research authorizes no publishing, forks, profile changes, wallet connection, or trades; those actions have not been performed as part of the review.
